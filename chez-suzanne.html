The Story Behind the Brand

More than a bakery.
A family memory.

The Name

Chez Suzanne is named after their grandmother — a woman who cooked with love and brought her grandchildren to La Baule every summer. The brand carries her name as a tribute, a thank you, a memory kept alive.

The Blue

The royal blue was not chosen at random. It is the exact shade of the beach cabins in La Baule — those striped cabanons where summers were spent, ice creams were eaten, and time stood still.

The Illustrations

Simple, slightly childlike drawings of croissants, cups, bread. They evoke the handmade drawings of childhood — naive, warm, full of life. Modern enough to stand out. Human enough to feel like home.

"Every design choice tells her story."
— Léa Hattab, Designer
The Challenge

Modernize without
losing the soul

01

A Single Outdated Logo

The brand had only one asset — an old logo that no longer reflected the warmth and quality of the bakery. No visual system, no consistency, no identity beyond a name on a sign.

02

Preserve the Emotional Core

Modernizing the brand could not mean erasing what made Chez Suzanne special. The nostalgia, the family story, the grandmother — all of it had to survive the redesign and feel even more present.

03

Build a Full System from One Logo

From a single starting point, I had to create a complete identity: logo variations, print materials, packaging, social media content — all cohesive, all consistent, all telling the same story.

What I Learned

Key
Takeaways

Storytelling is Design
The most powerful design decision on this project was not a color or a typeface — it was understanding the story first. Suzanne's summers in La Baule gave every visual choice its meaning and emotional weight.
Personal Projects Reveal Your True Style
Without a client brief to constrain me, I discovered my own design voice: warm, story-driven, modern but never cold. This project became a reference I return to when defining my aesthetic direction.
Consistency Builds Trust
Extending one logo into a full system taught me that a brand lives in the details — the same blue on a coffee cup, a loyalty card, and an Instagram post creates recognition that no single asset can achieve.
Constraints are Creative Allies
Starting with only an old logo and a family story, I had to build everything from first principles. The limitation forced me to be more intentional, more narrative, more thoughtful in every decision.
